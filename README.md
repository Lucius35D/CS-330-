# CS-330 Computational Graphics and Visualization

## Project Overview

This repository contains my final project for CS-330 Computational Graphics and Visualization at Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU). The project involved creating a 3D scene using C++ and OpenGL. My final scene depicts a desk setup with a coffee cup, notebook, pencil, and wooden table.

Throughout the course, I created 3D objects, applied textures, adjusted lighting, positioned objects, and implemented camera controls. The project helped me understand how different programming techniques come together to create a realistic 3D environment.

## How Do I Approach Designing Software?

This project helped me better understand how to design software by breaking a larger project into smaller, manageable tasks. One of the biggest skills I developed was combining basic 3D shapes to create more detailed objects. I also learned how important object placement, textures, and lighting are when designing a scene.

My design process involved deciding what objects I wanted in my scene, determining which shapes would work best, and making adjustments as I progressed through the milestones. For example, I made several changes to my notebook, pencil, and coffee cup to improve their appearance and positioning. I also experimented with different lighting settings to get the scene looking the way I wanted.

In future projects, I plan to continue breaking down complicated tasks into smaller steps. This approach makes it easier to identify problems, test solutions, and improve without redesigning the entire project.

## How Do I Approach Developing Programs?

Throughout this course, I learned new development strategies, especially for C++ and OpenGL. One important strategy was making changes to one part of my code at a time and testing the results before moving forward. This helped me understand how different parts of the program affected the overall scene.

Iteration played a major role in completing my project. At times, objects didn't look right, lighting was too bright or too dark, or textures needed adjustment. Instead of starting over, I made small changes, ran the program, and continued improving the results. One example was adjusting the lighting in my scene to balance brightness and color.

As I progressed through the course milestones, I became more comfortable reading and modifying existing code. At the beginning of the course, I was still learning how the program's different parts worked together. By the final project, I better understood how to troubleshoot problems, modify objects, and improve the overall appearance of my scene.
